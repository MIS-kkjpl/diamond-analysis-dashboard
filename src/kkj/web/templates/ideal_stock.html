{{ ui.section("How the suggestion is worked out") }}
The suggested number, in plain words

For every group of similar pieces (same purity, item and weight range) at every branch, the system first estimates how many will sell over the next months, mostly from the branch's own recent sales, with more weight on recent months. Where a group sells rarely, it leans on how the whole item sells.

The suggestion is then the largest of three numbers:

  1. Enough until new stock arrives: new stock takes about {{ rules.replacement_days }} days, so hold enough to keep selling until then, with a safety margin (bigger for the best sellers).
  2. Choice on the counter: customers like to choose, and branches that show more of a group sell more of it, so hold {{ rules.choice_months | num }} months of expected sales.
  3. At least one on display: {{ rules.display_min_pcs }} piece{{ "" if rules.display_min_pcs == 1 else "s" }} of anything the branch sold in the last {{ rules.display_min_months }} months.

Small differences (within {{ rules.ideal_band_pct | num }}%, or under {{ rules.ideal_min_change_pcs }} pieces) count as about right. The whole-company number is the branches added up; stock at head office counts as held. Your own number always wins over the suggestion. {% if can(request, "admin") %}These settings can be changed in Settings.{% endif %}

How accurate is the sales estimate? tested on the past
We went back to each of the last {{ origins }} month-ends, estimated the next {{ horizon }} months using only what was known then, and compared with what really sold. Lower is better. For a single group at a single branch the miss is larger ({{ "%.0f%%" | format(100 * fine_err) if fine_err is not none else "–" }}), because such a group sells only 0, 1 or 2 pieces in two months; the safety margin allows for that.
